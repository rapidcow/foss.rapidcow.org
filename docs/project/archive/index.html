the (frequently enough) project archive

My personal archive had been a million git-annex pointers (and growing!) living next to a collection of scripts in archive/* repositories since early 2024. The name was pretty striaghtforward: archive for the sake of preserving the past and the memories thereof. (and stop letting Google and Tencent have access to my entire childhood likeness (gaaaaah i’m so embarrassed. ///n///))

Despite the shared namespace, there has never been a collective “project archive” initiative? I would mutter that to myself in multiple occasions, but that doesn’t legitimize it in and of itself. I would flee the classroom with my MacBook to the library, attached to the power brick on my cube power strip, one old HDD with all the family photos, and one new HDD holding the replicated and checksummed files from the old one. As I was struggling to shape the identity of rapidcow.org, I was also rapidly moving data from collapsing points of failure that I was completely previously oblivious to, but couldn’t be clearer in hindsight. if I had just been a bit faster, a bit…

“Project archive” is still just a tongue-in-cheek though, eh. Besides, diary would be the only thing I was worried about three years ago, and I named that Project Perspective, after the journaling app I used itself DX… but most of it came from me getting tired of writing things on my phone as my writing exploded into long LISP-ian paragraphs of mess; and it made my thumb sore typing for hours long I wish I had a desktop app.

Comfort should not be the reason for change though, I believe. I have been reflecting the path I chose in a relentless race against time, against software obsolescence; like packing up as much as you can from a burning house slowly drowning in the throes of fire—but this is not really meant to be. Not barring the physical longevity of digital medium itself, data have none of the temporal qualities: they don’t degrade over time, they are cheap to replicate, and deterministic hashing makes sense. (I still can’t get over how confident we can be about the contents of a file, down to each bit, just from a fixed length of hash!) The urgency of data rescue (minus physical damage and degradation) comes from the longevity of software. Software, unlike the files they operate on, equire tending and taking care of like a garden. They need to evolve as we upgrade to new hardware supported by our PC manufacturer, which demand new operating systems.
